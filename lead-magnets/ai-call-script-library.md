### Script 1 — Cold Outbound Call (First Contact)
**Scenario:** A lead just submitted a form. Call within 60 seconds.

**① Exact words**
> "Hi, is this [First Name]? … Hey [First Name], it's [Agent Name] from [Company] — you just reached out on our site about [Service/Interest], so I wanted to get straight back to you while it's fresh. Did I catch you at an okay time? … Perfect. So I don't waste your time — what made you look into [Service/Interest] today?"
> *(Let them answer fully.)*
> "Got it — that's exactly what we help with. Mind if I ask two quick questions to see if we're even a fit? … Great."

**② Placeholders:** `[First Name]` `[Agent Name]` `[Company]` `[Service/Interest]`

**③ Why it works:** Calling within seconds of a form-fill is the biggest conversion lever there is — they're still at their desk, still thinking about you. Naming their exact enquiry proves it's not spam. "Okay time?" lowers defenses. Opening on *their* motivation makes it about them, not your pitch.

**④ Voice direction:** Warm and slightly upbeat, like you're glad to catch them. **Pace:** brisk on the intro, then *slow down* on "what made you look into this?" and **stop talking** — let the silence pull the answer out. Never sound relieved they answered; sound like you expected to.

### Script 2 — Warm Lead Follow-Up (Form Submitter / Prior Interest)
**Scenario:** They engaged before — downloaded something, replied, or asked for info.

**① Exact words**
> "Hi [First Name], it's [Agent Name] from [Company] — you grabbed our [Resource] on [Topic] a little while back, so I figured I'd reach out directly instead of emailing you again. Did you get a chance to look it over? … [If yes] Nice — what stood out? … [If no] No stress — that's actually why I called. I can give you the 60-second version right now. Want it?"

**② Placeholders:** `[First Name]` `[Agent Name]` `[Company]` `[Resource]` `[Topic]`

**③ Why it works:** Referencing a specific prior action signals attentiveness and continuity. The easy out ("no stress") removes guilt and keeps it alive. "The 60-second version" reframes the call as a favor, not an interrogation.

**④ Voice direction:** Familiar, like picking up with someone you've met. **Pace:** relaxed, conversational. Let your voice lift on "Want it?" so it lands as a genuine offer, not a closing line. Smile while you say "no stress" — it's audible.

### Script 3 — Appointment Confirmation
**Scenario:** A meeting is booked; confirm to kill no-shows.

**① Exact words**
> "Hi [First Name], quick one — it's [Agent Name] from [Company]. Just confirming your call with [Rep Name] on [Day] at [Time]. You're all set your end? … Perfect. You'll get a calendar invite and a reminder text. One thing that'll make the call most useful: [Prep Item]. Anything you want to make sure we cover? … Great — talk [Day]."

**② Placeholders:** `[First Name]` `[Agent Name]` `[Company]` `[Rep Name]` `[Day]` `[Time]` `[Prep Item]`

**③ Why it works:** A live verbal "yes" creates commitment and consistency, slashing no-shows. Asking what they want covered gets them invested. The prep item makes the meeting feel valuable, raising show-intent.

**④ Voice direction:** Efficient and friendly — this is a 30-second call, not a chat. **Pace:** quick, confident, no dead air. Land "talk [Day]" with warmth so it feels like a plan between people, not a system reminder.

### Script 4 — Voicemail (No Answer)
**Scenario:** Call goes to voicemail. Keep under 20 seconds.

**① Exact words**
> "Hi [First Name], it's [Agent Name] from [Company] — you just reached out about [Service/Interest], so I'm calling you straight back. I'll try you again shortly, but if it's easier, text or call me on [Callback Number] — comes right to me. Talk soon."

**② Placeholders:** `[First Name]` `[Agent Name]` `[Company]` `[Service/Interest]` `[Callback Number]`

**③ Why it works:** Short = heard in full. References their enquiry (not a cold pitch), sets the expectation of a callback so the next attempt isn't a surprise, and offers a low-friction reply path. "Comes right to me" makes it personal, not a call-center loop.

**④ Voice direction:** Calm, unrushed, lower energy than a live call — voicemails that race sound like robocalls. **Pace:** deliberate. Say the callback number *slowly* and, ideally, twice. End soft, not abrupt.

### Script 5 — Objection Handling: Price ("It's too expensive / what's it cost?")
**Scenario:** They raise cost before they see value.

**① Exact words**
> "Totally fair to ask up front. Before I throw out a number that might be too high *or* too low — what are you spending right now to *generate* a lead? … And roughly how many of those turn into customers? … Here's why I ask: most people we work with aren't overpaying for leads — they're *underworking* the ones they've already paid for. If even a few more converted because they got called in 60 seconds instead of two days, the system pays for itself. Want me to run those numbers with you?"

**② Placeholders:** *(none — pulls from discovery)*

**③ Why it works:** Reframes price from *cost* to *return*. Anchoring on current cost-per-lead and conversion makes the product a way to *recover* wasted spend, not a new expense. Ending on a question keeps control and moves toward the ROI conversation.

**④ Voice direction:** Unflinching and relaxed — price questions are won by *not* getting nervous. **Pace:** slow and even; never speed up defensively. A small, confident pause before "Here's why I ask" signals you've handled this a hundred times.

### Script 6 — Objection Handling: Timing ("Now's not a good time / call me in a few months")
**Scenario:** They try to defer.

**① Exact words**
> "Makes sense — no pressure. Can I ask, is it a *timing* thing or a *priority* thing? … [If timing] Cool — the reason I ask is this goes live in 48 hours and runs in the background, so there's no big project to find time for. It actually gives you time back. … [If priority] Fair. Most people bump it up the list once they realize every week without it, leads they already paid for go cold. How about this — 30 minutes now, and if the numbers don't make it urgent, we park it. Worst case, you've got a benchmark. Reasonable?"

**② Placeholders:** *(none)*

**③ Why it works:** Separates a real constraint from a soft brush-off so you handle the actual objection. Kills the "big project" fear. "Worst case you've got a benchmark" makes yes nearly risk-free.

**④ Voice direction:** Easygoing, zero desperation — the moment you push, you lose. **Pace:** matter-of-fact. Ask "timing or priority?" then **pause and genuinely wait**; their answer decides your branch. Land "Reasonable?" lightly, like an obvious yes.

---

### Script 7 — Objection Handling: Decision-Maker ("I need to talk to my partner / boss")
**Scenario:** They're not the sole decision-maker.

**① Exact words**
> "Smart — a call like this *should* have the right people in the room. Quick question so I set you up to win: when you bring it to [Decision-Maker], what's the first thing they'll want to know? … Got it. Let me give you exactly that, in plain numbers, so you're not stuck answering on the spot. Even better — want me to hop on a quick 20-minute call with [Decision-Maker] directly? I'll do the explaining, you just listen."

**② Placeholders:** `[Decision-Maker]`

**③ Why it works:** Respects the buying process instead of fighting it, building trust. Arming your champion with the likely first question makes them look good internally. Offering to join removes the broken-telephone risk of a secondhand pitch.

**④ Voice direction:** Collaborative, like you're on *their* side of the table. **Pace:** thoughtful, unhurried. Emphasize "set you up to win" — make them feel coached, not closed. No urgency here; urgency reads as manipulation when a third party's involved.

### Script 8 — Lead Qualification Script
**Scenario:** Screen any lead to score hot / warm / not-a-fit.

**① Exact words**
> "Mind if I ask a few quick questions to make sure we're actually a fit? Two minutes. … [1] What are you using right now to follow up with new leads? … [2] Roughly how many leads come in a month? … [3] How fast does someone usually get back to them? … [4] When a lead's ready to buy, what happens next on your end? … [5] If we could call every one of those leads in under a minute and book the good ones for you — is that something you'd want live in the next couple of weeks, or more of a 'someday' thing?"

**② Placeholders:** *(none — answers feed your scoring)*

**③ Why it works:** Five tight questions surface need, volume, speed gap, process gap, and urgency. Q5 doubles as a soft trial close — "next couple of weeks" vs "someday" instantly sorts hot from warm without pressure.

**④ Voice direction:** Curious and light, like a doctor taking history — not an interrogation. **Pace:** one question at a time, then **stop and listen**. Never stack two questions. Let answers breathe; the pauses are where they sell themselves.

### Script 9 — Meeting Booking Script
**Scenario:** Lead is qualified; lock a time.

**① Exact words**
> "Based on what you've told me, this is a strong fit — let's get you 30 minutes with [Rep Name] to map it to your exact setup. I've got [Day A] at [Time A] or [Day B] at [Time B] — which is easier? … Perfect, booking [Chosen Slot] now. You'll get a confirmation text and calendar invite within the minute. Quick favor: come with your monthly lead count and what you spend per lead — that's all [Rep Name] needs to show you the numbers. Sound good?"

**② Placeholders:** `[Rep Name]` `[Day A]` `[Time A]` `[Day B]` `[Time B]` `[Chosen Slot]`

**③ Why it works:** Two specific slots (not "when are you free?") remove decision friction and assume the booking. Instant text + invite cements commitment. The prep ask improves the meeting *and* raises show-rate — people show up when they've done homework.

**④ Voice direction:** Decisive and warm — you've earned this, so book it like it's natural. **Pace:** confident, no hedging. Offer the two slots crisply, then pause for their pick. Land "Sound good?" as a friendly full-stop, not a question you're unsure about.

### Script 10 — Follow-Up After a No-Show
**Scenario:** Booked lead didn't show. Call within 10 minutes — no guilt.

**① Exact words**
> "Hi [First Name], it's [Agent Name] from [Company] — we had a call booked for [Time] and I couldn't reach you, so I figured the day got away from you. Totally happens, no drama. I've still got the time blocked — want to just do it now while we're both here? … [If no] No problem — I've got [Day A] at [Time A] or [Day B] at [Time B], which works better? I'll lock it in and text you a reminder an hour before so it doesn't slip again."

**② Placeholders:** `[First Name]` `[Agent Name]` `[Company]` `[Time]` `[Day A]` `[Time A]` `[Day B]` `[Time B]`

**③ Why it works:** Calling fast catches them while they may still be free. The graceful excuse ("the day got away from you") removes shame — the #1 reason no-shows ghost. Offering to do it *now* converts a chunk on the spot; the reminder rescues the rest.

**④ Voice direction:** Genuinely no-blame — light, almost amused, never passive-aggressive. **Pace:** easy and warm. The whole job of your tone is to make them feel *relieved* you called, not caught. If they sound embarrassed, get lighter, not firmer.
