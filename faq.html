Lead follow-up and AI calling: your questions

Short answers, each linked to the guide that backs it up. General information, not legal advice.

Speed to lead

What is speed to lead?

The time between someone enquiring and your first attempt to contact them. It is the gap between the lead being created and the first call, not the time it takes to read the enquiry. Read the speed to lead guide.

How fast should you respond to a new lead?

As soon as you can, ideally within five minutes. The Lead Response Management study found the odds of making contact fell more than tenfold within the first hour. The data is American, so treat it as direction, not a forecast. If you miss the lead, send a short message straight away and try again at a time you state. See the study and its limits.

How fast should you respond to a Facebook lead?

The same principle applies to any enquiry from an ad: the person asked while their problem was in front of them, and they may be enquiring with several businesses. Get a phone notification the moment a lead arrives, name one person responsible, and ring within minutes. Our guide covers a manual five-minute process and where automation fills the gaps.

What is the average lead response time?

We cannot give you a reliable Australian figure. Widely repeated averages come from US studies or cannot be traced to a source, so we leave them out. The useful number is your own: measure the median time from enquiry to first call attempt for a week.

Does speed to lead matter more than lead quality?

They multiply each other. A fast call to a poor lead wastes the speed, and a good lead called two days later wastes the lead. If you have to choose where to start, response time is usually the cheaper one to fix.

Is an AI receptionist worth it for a small business?

It depends on how many calls you miss and what each one is worth. If a missed call is a lost job worth hundreds of dollars, a plan under $250 a month can pay for itself with one or two recovered jobs. If you rarely miss calls, it will not.

How it works

What is AI lead qualification?

An AI voice agent asks the questions you would ask, scores the lead hot, warm or not yet, and routes it. In our Outbound Call Engine, hot leads are transferred live to your team and warm leads are booked into your calendar. See how it works.

What is an AI live transfer?

The AI qualifies a lead on the phone, then hands the call to a person on your team while the lead is still on the line. We use it for hot leads. A booking is the alternative for warm ones.

What happens if a lead does not answer?

They get an instant SMS, and the engine retries over three days.

Is an AI receptionist the same as AI lead callback?

No. A receptionist answers calls people make to you. Lead callback works on enquiries that have already arrived and goes out to the lead. Which you need depends on whether your problem is unanswered calls or leads that go cold. Read the comparison.

Calling and messaging rules

Do I need consent to call or text a lead in Australia?

Consent comes first, and it depends on what the person agreed to when they enquired. ACMA says a call the person requested, made within a reasonable time, is generally not telemarketing, but a call that also sells or promotes is commercial. We treat every callback as needing consent. The Do Not Call Register and the Spam Act are separate regimes: the register is about telemarketing calls, and the Spam Act covers commercial SMS and email, including a working unsubscribe. Read ACMA's telemarketing and e-marketing guidance and take advice for your situation. This is general information, not legal advice. Read our compliance guide.

Who is responsible for AI disclosure and consent when I use a provider?

Check your contract. At least one Australian provider's FAQ puts responsibility for telling callers they are speaking to an AI, and for obtaining consent, on the customer. Ask for the answer in writing before you sign.

Is outbound AI calling legal in Australia?

We found no ban on AI voices making calls, and the Do Not Call Register site says synthetic-voice calls are allowed under the telemarketing rules. The same rules apply as for a person: consent or a number not on the register, permitted hours, identifying the business and purpose, ending the call on request, and caller ID. Read the compliance guide.

What hours can telemarketers call in Australia?

Weekdays 9am to 8pm and Saturdays 9am to 5pm, at the person's usual residential address. No calls on Sundays or national public holidays, unless the person has expressly agreed in advance to that particular call. The source is the Telemarketing and Research Calls Industry Standard 2017.

Does an AI caller have to say it is an AI?

As of October 2026 we could not find an Australian law that requires it outright. Government guidance says people should know when they are interacting with AI, and the Australian Consumer Law bans misleading conduct, so we recommend saying so in the first sentence. See the sources.

Do I have to tell people a call is recorded?

The federal law treats recording a call without the knowledge of the person making it as interception, and state laws differ, so the simple practice that works everywhere is to say at the start that the call is recorded.
