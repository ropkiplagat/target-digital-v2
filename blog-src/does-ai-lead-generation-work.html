Does AI lead generation work? What it can and can't do

Published 6 October 2026 · Sources read on 5 and 6 October 2026 · By Target Digital · 9 minute read

AI lead generation works for some jobs and not others, and the honest answer depends on which job you mean. AI is good at replying to a new enquiry in seconds, asking a few questions and booking a time. It cannot make people want what you sell, and the claims some vendors make about it are hard to check.

This is a buyer's guide from a company that sells AI follow-up, so we say where we are biased. It covers what the phrase means, which parts have evidence behind them, the legal rules that apply to AI exactly as they do to people, how to read a vendor's numbers, and a simple way to test it for 30 days. We have no verified results from our own outbound engine to quote yet, so we quote none.

What AI lead generation means

People use the phrase for four different jobs, and they have different track records:

  • Capture: getting enquiries in the first place, through ads, search and referrals. AI can help write and test ads, but the demand still has to exist.
  • Qualification: asking an enquiry the questions you would ask and scoring it. AI can do this by phone, text or chat.
  • Follow-up: contacting every enquiry quickly and persistently, and booking the good ones into a calendar.
  • Outreach: contacting people who have not asked to hear from you. This is where the legal risk is highest.

Most of the value that is easy to defend sits in the middle two. A business that already gets enquiries and loses them to slow replies has a problem AI can fix. A business with no enquiries has a different problem.

What the evidence says

The best-documented finding is about speed, not about AI. The Lead Response Management study, which covers more than 15,000 web-form leads and more than 100,000 call attempts across six companies, found the odds of making contact fell more than tenfold within the first hour. The study does not say where its six companies operate, the write-up comes from a company that also sells lead distribution software, and the page carries a 2022 footer and unrelated advertising, so treat it as direction. Our guide to speed to lead covers it in detail.

That finding supports the idea behind AI follow-up: if waiting makes leads harder to reach, a system that responds in seconds, at any hour, removes the wait. It does not prove that any particular product raises your sales. We could not find independent, audited results for AI lead generation products in Australia. Treat any figure you see as the vendor's claim until you can check it.

The legal rules apply to AI exactly as to people

AI changes how fast a business can contact people, not what it is allowed to do. Three points catch buyers out.

  • Calls and texts need consent. Telemarketing calls, including those using AI voices, must follow the Do Not Call Register Act and the telemarketing Industry Standard, and texts must follow the Spam Act. Our compliance guide sets out consent, hours and what the caller must say.
  • Bought and scraped lists are risky. ACMA's guidance on spam says you cannot use or supply a list created with address-harvesting software, and that if you buy or use a marketing list you are still responsible for making sure you have consent for each address. It also says that even when someone else sends your messages, you must still have consent.
  • An AI voice must not mislead. The consumer law ban on misleading conduct applies to AI callers, which we cover in our guide to AI caller disclosure.

A vendor that offers to "generate leads" by contacting a purchased list is selling something that depends on consent you may not have. Ask where the list came from and how consent was recorded.

How to read a vendor's claims

  1. Define the unit. What exactly is a lead, a qualified lead and a booked appointment? Ranges vary because definitions do, and our guide to lead generation pricing shows how widely vendor figures differ.
  2. Ask for the source. Is a statistic from an audited report, a client who agreed to be named, or a marketing page?
  3. Check consistency. Do the numbers on the home page match the numbers on other pages?
  4. Ask who owns the data, the phone numbers and the accounts if you leave.
  5. Ask to hear real calls, including ones that went badly.
  6. Ask how consent is captured and stored, and who is responsible if it is wrong.

A 30-day test you can run

  1. Baseline. For two weeks, record the time from each enquiry to your first call attempt, and how many enquiries became a conversation and a booking.
  2. Switch on the follow-up for one lead source, with a consent tick box on the form.
  3. Measure the same three numbers for the next two weeks: median response time, conversations, bookings.
  4. Listen to a sample of calls and read the texts.
  5. Judge it on bookings and sales, not on the number of calls made.

AI lead generation: common questions

Can AI do lead generation?

It can do parts of it: replying fast, qualifying, following up and booking. It cannot create demand, and outreach to people who have not asked to hear from you carries legal risk.

Is AI lead generation worth it?

It depends on how many enquiries you receive and how many you lose to slow follow-up. If you get few enquiries, other work comes first. If you get many and reply late, it is worth testing.

Is AI lead generation legal?

The tools are legal. What you do with them must follow the same consent, calling-hours and unsubscribe rules as any other marketing, and those rules apply to AI voices and texts. This is general information, not legal advice.

How do I know if a vendor's results are real?

Ask for the definition, the source and the date, and check that the numbers agree across the vendor's own pages. If you cannot check a figure, treat it as a claim.
