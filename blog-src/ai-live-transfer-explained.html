AI live transfer: what it is and when your business needs it

Published 6 October 2026 · Provider details read on 5 October 2026 · By Target Digital · 7 minute read

AI live transfer is when an AI agent qualifies a lead on the phone and then connects the call to a person on your team while the lead is still on the line. It turns a form submission into a live conversation with your salesperson in minutes, without anyone on your team dialling.

It is not the right choice for every business or every lead. This guide explains how it works, the difference between a warm and a cold transfer, what goes wrong when nobody picks up, and when booking an appointment is the better outcome. It describes our own engine and the options other providers list, and where we are giving our view we say so.

How AI live transfer works

In our AI Outbound Call Engine, the sequence runs like this:

  1. A lead submits a form.
  2. The lead gets an instant SMS and an AI phone call.
  3. The AI asks your qualifying questions and scores the lead.
  4. Hot leads are transferred live to your sales team.
  5. Warm leads are booked into your calendar.
  6. No answer: the engine retries over three days.

The transfer happens inside the call. The lead does not hang up, wait for a callback or fill in another form. If you have no sales team, the engine books every qualified lead for you to take yourself instead. You can hear a call yourself: the demo calls your phone, with your consent.

Warm transfer and cold transfer

Providers use two terms. AiDial's platform description is a useful example because it lists both:

  • Warm transfer: the AI briefs the person receiving the call with the conversation so far, so they pick up knowing who the lead is and what they said.
  • Cold transfer: the call is connected without a briefing, with fallback options if the destination does not answer.

AiDial also lists notifications by SMS and email when a transfer fails, voicemail detection with automatic retry, and configurable timeouts. These are exactly the details to ask any provider about. In our view a warm transfer is worth asking for, because the person who takes the call starts with context instead of asking the lead to repeat themselves.

What happens when nobody answers

A live transfer needs a live person. Plan for the failures before you switch it on:

  • Nobody picks up. The AI should keep the lead engaged and offer a booking or a named callback time rather than letting the line ring out.
  • It is outside team hours. Do not transfer to a phone nobody is watching. Route the lead to a booking instead.
  • The lead hangs up while waiting. Keep the wait short. If the transfer fails, send the lead a text and alert your team so they can ring back at once.
  • The team member is already on a call. Use a fallback chain of two or three people, then booking.

Treat the booking as the safety net. A lead who cannot reach a person should still end the call with a time in your diary.

The compliance side of a transfer

A transfer does not suspend the calling rules. The telemarketing Industry Standard 2017 still applies to the whole call: permitted hours, the opening disclosures, a mechanism for a synthetic-voice call to reach more information, and ending the call at once if the person asks. Three practical points follow:

  • Check consent before the AI dials, not after the transfer. Our guide to consent to call from a lead form explains how.
  • Say it is an AI at the start. The reasons are in our guide to AI caller disclosure.
  • If calls are recorded, say so at the start, so the transferred part of the call is covered too. See our compliance guide for recording.

What the speed research says about the handover

The Lead Response Management study found that the odds of qualifying a lead fell fourfold between a 5-minute and a 10-minute response, and that the odds of making contact fell more than tenfold within the first hour. The data is American, covers six companies and comes from a vendor of lead distribution software, so read it as direction. The relevance to a transfer is simple: the AI call already answers the speed question by reaching the lead within minutes, and a live transfer puts a person on the line before the lead's attention moves on, instead of leaving a booking for later.

Questions to ask a provider about transfers

Capabilities differ, and the failure cases are where they show. AiDial's published feature list is a useful template for what to ask, because it names the details most providers leave out:

  • Destinations. AiDial lists transfers to SIP, PSTN or mobile destinations. Can the provider reach the phone your team actually answers?
  • Timeouts and retries. AiDial lists a configurable transfer timeout and retry logic. How long does the lead wait before the system gives up?
  • Voicemail detection. AiDial lists voicemail detection with automatic retry. What stops a lead being transferred into a voicemail box?
  • Fallback chains. AiDial describes cold transfers with configurable fallback chains. Who is tried second and third?
  • Failure alerts. AiDial lists SMS and email notifications when a transfer fails or is unavailable. Does your team hear about a missed hot lead straight away?
  • Briefing. AiDial describes warm transfers with a conversation briefing. What does the receiving person see or hear before they speak?

These are one provider's published features, not a standard. Ask every provider, including us, to show each one working on a test call.

Test a transfer before go-live

These steps are our recommendations. A transfer that works once in a demo can still fail on a Friday afternoon.

  1. Call the system as a lead and ask to be transferred, then time how long it takes from the AI's last sentence to a person's voice.
  2. Let the transfer ring out, so you hear what the lead hears when nobody answers.
  3. Send the transfer to a phone that is on voicemail.
  4. Answer a transfer on a busy line, to see whether a second person is tried.
  5. Say "stop calling me" during the AI's questions and confirm the call ends at once.
  6. Ask "are you a real person?" and check the answer is honest. See our guide to AI caller disclosure.
  7. Check the transfer details arrive with the lead record in your CRM or inbox.

What to pass to your team at the moment of transfer

The person who takes the call should know, without asking, who the lead is and what they said. A useful minimum is the lead's name and number, what they enquired about, the answers to your qualifying questions, the time of the original enquiry, and whether the lead gave consent to be called and texted. Putting consent status in front of the salesperson also stops a follow-up text going to someone who did not agree to one.

Recording a transferred call

If the system records calls, say so at the start, so the recording notice covers the part of the call after the handover. Under the federal Telecommunications (Interception and Access) Act 1979, interception consists of listening to or recording a call without the knowledge of the person making it, and state surveillance laws differ. Queensland's Invasion of Privacy Act 1971, for example, does not prohibit a party to a private conversation from recording it, but it limits what that party can later do with the recording. AI live transfer: common questions

Is live transfer the same as a booked appointment?

No. A live transfer connects the lead to a person during the call. A booking puts a time in your calendar for later. Many setups use both: transfer the hot leads and book the warm ones.

What happens if nobody answers the transfer?

That depends on how the system is set up, so ask. A good setup tries other people, then falls back to a booking and alerts your team. In our engine, leads who do not answer the AI call are retried over three days.

Does the lead know it is an AI?

They should. We recommend the agent says so in its first sentence, and our guide on AI caller disclosure explains why.

Do I need a sales team for this?

No. Without one, every qualified lead is booked for you to take yourself. The transfer only matters if someone can take the call.
