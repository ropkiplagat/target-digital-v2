How to follow up Facebook lead ads in five minutes

Published 6 October 2026 · Meta documentation read on 6 October 2026 · By Target Digital · 8 minute read

To follow up Facebook lead ads in five minutes, you need three things in place before the first lead arrives: an instant alert, one person responsible for it, and a consent record that lets you call. Most businesses have none of the three, so leads from paid ads wait in a list until someone checks it.

This guide gives you three setups, from a manual process to an AI callback, plus the delay in Meta's own system that you need to plan around. The case for speed is in our guide to speed to lead: the research is American and its multiples are a direction, but waiting makes every lead harder to reach.

Know what Meta does with your leads

Three facts from Meta's documentation shape every setup below.

  • Leads expire. Meta says you have 90 days from the time a lead submits their form to download their information, and recommends downloading often or using a CRM so you do not lose the data.
  • Real-time delivery is not instant. Meta's developer documentation says its real-time lead updates arrive through webhooks, and that on success the pings occur "with a delay of up to a few minutes". If your target is five minutes, part of that budget is already spent before your system sees the lead.
  • Many CRMs connect directly. Meta keeps a list of CRM integrations for lead ads, and Leads Center in Meta Business Suite lets you organise and download leads yourself.

Each lead record carries a created time. Use it as the start of your clock.

Three ways to follow up Facebook lead ads fast

Setup 1: manual, with a phone alert

This works for a handful of leads a day and costs nothing but discipline.

  1. Connect your leads to a CRM or another tool that can alert a person's phone the moment a lead lands. Do not rely on checking Leads Center.
  2. Name one owner for every shift, including weekends. Their only job on a new lead is to ring within five minutes.
  3. Call first. If there is no answer, send a short text that says who you are, that you saw their enquiry, and when you will try again.
  4. Log the time of the first attempt against the lead.

Its weakness is people. The owner is on a job, in a meeting or asleep, and the lead waits.

Setup 2: automatic routing and acknowledgement

Connect the form to your CRM using Meta's integration list, then let rules do the routing: the lead goes to the right person, an alert fires, and an acknowledgement message goes out. This closes the gap between the lead arriving and a human noticing.

It does not remove the human. Someone still has to make the call. And the acknowledgement text needs the same consent as any commercial message under the Spam Act, so build the consent box first (next section).

Setup 3: AI callback

The lead triggers an instant text and an AI voice call that asks your qualifying questions. Hot leads transfer live to your team, warm leads are booked into your calendar, and leads who do not answer are retried over three days. This is what our AI Outbound Call Engine does, and it works on a lead from an ad form the same way it does for a website form.

It suits businesses where leads arrive faster, or at more hours, than people can cover, and where the first conversation is a short set of questions. If your sale is long and relationship-led, keep a person on the first call and use automation for the acknowledgement and the booking. You can hear it call your own phone before you decide.

Get consent before the first call

Speed does not suspend the rules. A person who submits an instant form has not necessarily agreed to a phone call or text from an AI agent. The fix is a consent checkbox in the form, which we cover in does a Facebook lead form count as consent to call. For the number checks, see calling leads on the Do Not Call Register.

Two more rules shape the timing. Calls are only allowed on weekdays 9am to 8pm and Saturdays 9am to 5pm at the person's usual residential address, with none on Sundays or national public holidays, under the telemarketing Industry Standard. So a lead that arrives at 10pm gets an acknowledgement and a call at the next permitted time, not a call at 10.01pm. The full list is in our compliance guide, and ACMA publishes the same hours on its telemarketing page.

Meta also says to message people only for the purpose for which you collected their details. A quote request is not a marketing list.

Measure the one number that matters

Take the lead's created time and the time of your first call attempt, and track the median gap each week. Use the median, because one slow Friday should not hide a good week. If the median is over five minutes, the problem is almost always the alert or the ownership, not the ad.

Mistakes that cost leads

  • Leaving leads in Meta and forgetting the 90-day expiry.
  • No alert, so the lead waits until someone looks.
  • No owner for evenings and weekends, which is when many people browse.
  • Calling without consent or outside permitted hours.
  • Using the details for an unrelated promotion.

Follow up Facebook lead ads: common questions

How fast should you respond to a Facebook lead?

As fast as you can, with five minutes as the target. The research behind that is American, so treat it as direction, and remember Meta's own delivery can add a few minutes.

How do I connect Facebook lead ads to my CRM?

Meta lists the CRM systems that integrate with lead ads. Check whether yours is on the list, then follow your CRM's own setup. If it is not listed, a developer can use Meta's webhook updates to pass leads to another tool.

What about leads that arrive at night?

Send an acknowledgement if the person has consented to texts, and call at the next permitted hour unless they asked for a call at that time.

Do I have to run ads to use AI follow-up?

No. It works on website forms, ad forms and missed calls alike. Ads produce more enquiries for it to handle.
