Does a Facebook lead form count as consent to call?

Published 6 October 2026 · Sources read on 6 October 2026 · By Target Digital · 7 minute read

A Facebook lead form is not consent to call a lead on its own, but it can capture that consent if you add it. The person who types a phone number into an instant form has asked for something from your ad. They have not necessarily agreed to a phone call, an AI voice or a stream of texts.

This guide covers what Meta's form does and does not capture, what Australian regulators expect, and the exact setting in Meta's form builder that fixes it. It is general information, not legal advice. For the wider rules on hours, disclosure and recording, read our guide to AI outbound calling compliance in Australia.

What a Facebook lead form captures by default

Meta's own help centre leaves consent to the advertiser. Its requirements and recommendations page says your privacy policy and terms are where you "capture whatever consent you feel you need from the user to comply with the law". Meta recommends telling people why you are collecting their contact information and how you will use or share it, and says to message people only for the purpose for which you collected the information.

So Meta gives you the form and the checkbox tools. The decision about what the person has agreed to sits with you.

What counts as consent to call a lead in Australia

Two regimes matter. Calls fall under the Do Not Call Register Act and the telemarketing standard. Texts fall under the Spam Act. Both turn on consent, and ACMA is specific about what is weak and what is strong.

ACMA's statement of expectations says that if a consumer visits a website or sends an email to a business, that is unlikely to constitute consent to inclusion on a marketing list. It also says the ACMA "recommends using express consent as it involves a clear and unambiguous choice made by a consumer", and that consent more than three months old becomes stale unless the person agreed to a longer period.

For texts, ACMA's Spam Act guidance sets three rules: consent, identify yourself accurately, and a working unsubscribe honoured within five working days.

Put together, a person who submits your instant form has given you their number and asked about your service. The Do Not Call Register site says express consent can be given "by completing a web form requesting contact", so a form that asks for a callback can count. Whether it covers a call from an AI agent and follow-up texts is arguable, because a generic enquiry does not say so. Specific express consent removes the argument.

What if the call is one the person asked for?

ACMA's statement of expectations says "Calls made by businesses that are requested by a consumer and are made within a reasonable time after the request is made are generally not telemarketing calls". That helps a prompt callback that only does what the form asked for. It does not help if the call also sells or promotes something, because ACMA says a call with more than one purpose is commercial if one purpose is to sell or promote, and that commercial calls using AI voices are telemarketing calls. "Generally" also leaves room for argument. A consent box settles it, and it is the only thing that covers the follow-up texts.

The fix: add a consent checkbox to the instant form

Meta's custom disclaimer feature is the tool. By Meta's description, a custom disclaimer is a legal notice or consent disclaimer shown when people view your form, and you can include checkboxes in it. Two details matter:

  • When you add a disclaimer to your instant form, people are required to agree to submit the form.
  • You can add more than one checkbox with "Add new consent", and you can mark a checkbox as optional.

That second point lets you do the right thing. Put the call-and-text permission in its own checkbox and mark it optional, so the person makes a real choice. A person who unticks it can still submit the enquiry, and you follow up in a way that does not need that consent. This is our recommendation, not a rule: a box the person must tick to submit is harder to call a free choice. Check your approach with your adviser.

Wording to adapt

"Yes, [your business] may call and text me on this number about my enquiry, including using an AI assistant. I can opt out at any time." Link your privacy policy in the form, and say what the person will receive and from whom. Name the business exactly as it will appear when it calls or texts.

Keep a record you can show

Forms get edited. Six months from now you will not remember what the checkbox said in March. For each lead, keep:

  • the exact consent wording and the date the form version went live;
  • whether the person ticked the box, stored with the lead in your CRM, so check that the answer comes through with the lead data;
  • the time of submission and the phone number.

The Do Not Call Register's industry guide says to get and record permission, because you need to provide evidence to ACMA if asked.

What to do with leads who did not tick the box

  1. Do not call a number on the Do Not Call Register without consent. Our guide to calling leads on the Do Not Call Register explains how to check a list.
  2. Do not text promotional follow-ups. Texts need consent under the Spam Act.
  3. Reply in the channel they used. Acknowledge the enquiry and invite them to book a time or call you.
  4. Stay inside the purpose. Meta says to message people only for the purpose for which you collected their details. A quote request does not become a marketing list.

What this means for speed to lead

Fast follow-up is the point of the whole exercise, and the speed to lead research explains why minutes matter. A consent box costs the person one tap and lets the call happen within minutes, lawfully. A form without it forces you to choose between slow, cautious follow-up and calls you may not be allowed to make.

Consent to call: common questions

Do I need consent to text leads under the Spam Act?

Yes. ACMA's rules for commercial messages are consent, accurate identification of the sender, and a working unsubscribe honoured within five working days. Consent can be express or inferred, and you must be able to show it.

What should my lead form say?

Say who will contact the person, by which channels (call and text), about what, and how to opt out. Put it in its own checkbox rather than burying it in the privacy policy, and link the policy.

Can I call a Facebook lead straight away?

If you hold express consent that covers calls, and the call is inside permitted hours, yes. Hours are weekdays 9am to 8pm and Saturdays 9am to 5pm at the person's usual residential address, with no Sunday or national public holiday calls.

Does consent expire?

ACMA says consent to receive telemarketing that is more than three months old becomes stale, unless the person agreed to a longer period.
