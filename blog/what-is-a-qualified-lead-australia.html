What is a qualified lead? How to define one

Published 12 October 2026 · By Target Digital · 6 minute read

What is a qualified lead? It is an enquiry that you have checked against your own rules and found worth a sales conversation. A raw lead is someone who put their hand up. A qualified lead is someone who put their hand up and also fits what you sell, wants it soon enough to matter, and can say yes.

There is no one definition every business uses. Each business has to write its own, and the sooner you do, the less you will argue about it later.

What is a qualified lead in plain terms?

Think of two enquiries that arrive on the same morning. One is a person who needs a job done next week, is in your service area and owns the property. The other filled in a form for a free guide and has not replied since.

Both are leads. Only the first is qualified. The word "qualified" just means someone has asked a few questions and the answers say "worth your time".

That check can be done by a person on the phone, by a form with the right fields, or by an AI asking questions. What matters is that the rules are written down before anyone starts asking.

What is a qualified lead versus a lead and a booked appointment?

These three terms get mixed up, usually in the sales pitch. Keep them apart:

  • Lead: someone who has shown interest and left contact details. Nothing has been checked.
  • Qualified lead: a lead whose answers meet your written criteria.
  • Booked appointment: a qualified lead who has agreed a time with you.

A business can have plenty of leads, few qualified leads and almost no appointments. Each step filters the one before it, so ask which of the three a number refers to before you act on it.

What do most definitions check?

Sales teams have used many frameworks over the years. This is our framing, not an official standard. We find four questions cover most service businesses:

  1. Need. Do they want the thing you actually sell?
  2. Fit. Are they in your area, and is the job within what you take on, including a budget that is realistic for it?
  3. Timing. Do they want it now, this month, or "one day"?
  4. Decision. Is this person able to say yes, or do they have to ask someone else?

You do not need all four to be perfect. You need to decide which ones are non-negotiable for your business. A plumber may only care about need, area and timing. A business with long, high-value jobs may care a great deal about who decides.

How do you write your own definition?

Set aside ten minutes and fill in this template. Use plain words, because your staff and any vendor you work with will have to apply it.

  1. We sell: (one line)
  2. We work in: (suburbs, regions, or "anywhere")
  3. A job is too small or too big when: (your own limits)
  4. Ready now means: (for example, wants it within a set time)
  5. The person must be: (owner, tenant, decision-maker, or someone who can pass it on)
  6. Automatically not qualified: (for example, outside the area, just browsing)
  7. A qualified lead is a person who meets points 1 to 5 and none of point 6.

Then test it on the last few enquiries you received. If you would have called every one of them, the rules are too loose. If you would have skipped people you were happy to work with, they are too tight.

Why do sales and marketing argue about it?

Whoever generates the enquiries wants the definition wide, because it makes their numbers look good. Whoever follows them up wants it narrow, because they are the ones who ring the dead ends.

Neither side is wrong. The fix is a single written definition that both teams, or the owner and a contractor, sign off on. If you pay someone for leads, this matters even more, because the definition decides what you are paying for. How can a vendor's "qualified" differ from yours?

When a supplier promises "qualified leads", the word is doing a lot of work. Before you sign, ask:

  • What exactly does a lead have to say or do to count as qualified?
  • Who decided those criteria, you or them?
  • Can I see how each lead was checked, such as the answers or a recording?
  • What happens to a lead that fails your check? Is it still billed?
  • Is the number you report leads, qualified leads or booked appointments?

If the answers are vague, treat "qualified" as marketing language. We cover the same caution in does AI lead generation work.

Where does AI qualification help, and where should a person decide?

AI tools are often used for the repetitive front end of qualification, where the same questions need asking each time. A person should still decide what the answers mean.

It is a poor fit for judgement calls. If a job is complex, high-stakes or relationship-led, a person should make the call about whether it is worth pursuing. A sensible setup uses AI to gather answers and a human to decide on the borderline ones. FAQ

What is a qualified lead in simple words?

It is an enquiry that has been checked against your own criteria and judged worth a sales conversation. The criteria usually cover need, fit, timing and who makes the decision.

What is the difference between a lead and a qualified lead?

A lead is anyone who shows interest and leaves contact details. A qualified lead is one whose answers meet your written rules. Qualification is the filter between the two.

Is a qualified lead the same as a booked appointment?

No. A booked appointment is a qualified lead who has agreed a time. Some qualified leads never get that far, so the numbers should be counted separately.

Who decides what counts as qualified?

You do. There is no standard that applies to every business. Write your own definition and make sure anyone supplying or following up leads uses it.
